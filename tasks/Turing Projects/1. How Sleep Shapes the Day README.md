# How Sleep Shapes the Day

## Project Overview

This project explores how sleep quality, time in bed, and wake-up mood relate to each other using a small personal sleep dataset.

The goal was to practice the full data analysis process on an everyday-life question: collect or use simple personal data, clean it, summarize it, visualize it, and turn the findings into a clear story.

## Main Deliverable

- `1. How Sleep Shapes the Day.xlsx`: Excel workbook with the analysis, charts, processed data, and source sleep records.

## Assignment Focus

The task followed the six-step data analysis process:

1. Ask: define a personal problem or question.
2. Prepare: choose and organize the data needed.
3. Process: clean and structure the data.
4. Analyze: summarize the data and look for patterns.
5. Share: present the findings clearly with visuals.
6. Act: explain what action could be taken from the findings.

## Workbook Structure

The Excel workbook contains these sheets:

- `Summary & Charts`: main story, findings, and visual summary.
- `Processed Data`: calculated metrics and summary tables.
- `Mood Distribution`: mood count visualization.
- `Sleep Quality by Moods`: comparison of sleep quality by wake-up mood.
- `Sleep Quality Over Time`: trend view of sleep quality.
- `Sleep Hours and Sleep Quality`: relationship between time in bed and sleep quality.
- `Sleeping Data`: original sleep records used for the analysis.

## Key Findings

- Good wake-up moods appeared on 21 out of 31 recorded mornings, or about 68%.
- Neutral moods appeared on 8 mornings.
- Unknown moods appeared on 2 mornings.
- Average sleep quality was about 77.6%.
- Good-mood days had higher average sleep quality than neutral or unknown mood days.
- Longer time in bed did not always guarantee better sleep quality, so sleep quality and mood needed to be compared together rather than using sleep hours alone.
