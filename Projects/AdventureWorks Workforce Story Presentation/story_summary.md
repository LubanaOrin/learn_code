# Story Summary

## Audience

The presentation is written for senior leadership reviewing workforce structure after a merger.

## Core Message

AdventureWorks has a stable active workforce snapshot, but the workforce structure is concentrated, hiring has been uneven, internal mobility is limited, and missing HR lifecycle data makes long-term workforce decisions harder.

## Main Evidence

- The dataset contains **290 active employees**.
- Production holds more than **60%** of employees.
- More than 10 departments have fewer than 10 employees.
- Hiring activity is volatile, with a large spike in 2009 followed by decline.
- Internal transfers are nearly absent, with only one recorded transfer per active department.
- Sales tenure is lower than most departments at **1.44 years**.
- Overall gender representation is about **71% male / 29% female**.
- Attrition appears as **0%**, but this is likely caused by missing termination and exit fields rather than proof of no employee exits.

## Main Risk

The company may be overdependent on Production while underinvesting in technical, quality, and supporting functions. At the same time, missing HR lifecycle data prevents reliable attrition and retention analysis.

## Recommendations

1. Rebalance headcount across critical and technical functions.
2. Replace reactive hiring cycles with forecast-led workforce planning.
3. Create cross-functional mobility pathways between Production, QA, and Engineering.
4. Set measurable diversity targets over a 3-year period.
5. Improve HR data completeness by tracking termination date, reason for exit, transfer reason, and standardized date fields.
